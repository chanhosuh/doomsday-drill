# Sources and Attribution

## Conway's Original Article

John Horton Conway, **Tomorrow is the Day After Doomsday**, *Eureka* 36,
October 1973, pp. 28-31. Published by The Archimedeans, the mathematics student
society of the University of Cambridge.

- [Readable transcription by Simon Plantinga](https://simonplantinga.nl/posts/conway-doomsday/),
  the default destination of the app's Reference button.
- [Original journal scan, preserved by the Internet Archive](https://web.archive.org/web/20240907031643/https://www.archim.org.uk/eureka/archive/Eureka-36.pdf#page=31).
  The article occupies PDF pages 31-34, corresponding to printed pages 28-31.
  The full issue is approximately 43 MB; the transcription is lighter to open.
- [The Archimedeans' current publications page](https://archim.soc.srcf.net/publications/),
  which links to the publisher's archive of past issues. Look for issue 36.

The archived scan begins with a publisher-added cover stating that the work is
published under [Creative Commons Attribution 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).
It credits The Archimedeans and the Betty & Gordon Moore Library, Cambridge.
The README's short quotation is taken from Conway's article, with attribution.
The scan is linked, not bundled in this repository. The app's code is separately
licensed under [MIT](../LICENSE); that license does not relicense Conway's article
or the README quotation.

The former publisher URL, `https://www.archim.org.uk/eureka/archive/Eureka-36.pdf`,
returned 404 when checked on September 6, 2026. The archived scan and transcription
were both verified on that date.

Conway's discussion of remembering additional Doomsdays, saying the year
calculation aloud, and making the calendar familiar informs this project.
The app's worked explanations are generated for each question; they are not a
transcription of the paper. Practice dates use the Gregorian calendar, including
for historical dates. Conway also discusses Julian dates and calendar changes,
which the drill does not model.

## Conway On Video

[*John H. Conway - Doomsday, part 1*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T_nQG-Bzxsg),
published on YouTube by the Mathematical Association of America.
Conway demonstrates the calendar calculation and explains the method himself.
The link starts at the beginning so readers see the demonstration first.

The video is linked, not bundled; the article's Creative Commons notice above
does not apply to the video.

## Odd + 11

Chamberlain Fong and Michael K. Walters,
[*Methods for Accelerating Conway's Doomsday Algorithm (part 2)*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.0765),
2010, arXiv:1010.0765.

The app presents their Odd + 11 method as an alternative for computing the year
shift. It is a later contribution, not a technique from Conway's 1973 article.

For another overview, see [the Doomsday rule on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doomsday_rule).
